Current Projects

What To Do When Your Phone Gets Stolen

A Systematic Evaluation of Online Advice for Phone-Theft Victims

Research Fellow · May 2026 – Present · USENIX Security 2027 (in prep)
Advisors: Prof. Lucy Simko, Prof. Yasemin Acar (Paderborn University)

Co-leading a research team evaluating the quality, coverage, and actionability of online advice available to phone theft victims. We designed a search protocol emulating how a victim would seek help — authoring 90 queries and controlling for time, location, and personalization — then applied reflexive thematic analysis to build a source × code matrix exposing systematic coverage gaps.

Key findings: advice protects the device but not the person; emotional support is nearly absent from official sources; and no single source is comprehensive. Supported by the Andrew W. Mellon Foundation and CyberNYC REU funding.

Presenting at Barnard College Summer Research Institute, 2026

Past Projects

Auto Cookie Rejector

A Chrome Extension for Automated Consent Refusal

Course Research Project · Jan – May 2026 · Advisor: Prof. Lucy Simko

Built a Manifest V3 Chrome extension that detects cookie consent interfaces and automatically selects the reject option. Engineered resilient DOM querying with fallback logic for dynamic loading, iframes, and site-specific implementations. Connected technical findings back to dark-patterns research: the interfaces hardest to automate away often correspond to the most manipulative consent designs.

Cookie Consent Interfaces

A Qualitative Study of Dark Patterns and User Decision-Making

Course Research Project · Sep – Dec 2025 · Advisor: Prof. Lucy Simko

Investigated how interface design and dark patterns shape user privacy decisions on cookie consent banners. Conducted semi-structured interviews, developed an iterative codebook, and performed thematic analysis. Identified recurring manipulative design mechanisms — unequal button salience, forced scrolling, and pre-checked defaults — and characterized how each undermines the voluntariness that consent regulation presumes.
